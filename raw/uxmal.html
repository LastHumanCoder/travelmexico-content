Uxmal: The Quieter Mayan Site

A UNESCO World Heritage city of Puuc stonework, an hour south of Merida, with a fraction of the crowds.

The short answer

Uxmal is a UNESCO World Heritage Mayan city in Yucatan, about an hour south of Merida. It is famous for elegant Puuc-style architecture, the rounded Pyramid of the Magician, and far smaller crowds than Chichen Itza. Arrive early, budget two to three hours, and pair it with the nearby Puuc Route.

The Uxmal ruins sit about an hour south of Merida in Yucatan state. Where Chichen Itza pulls in thousands of day-trippers, Uxmal stays calm enough that you can walk among the finest Puuc-style stonework in the Maya world at your own pace. Uxmal (say it "oosh-mahl") was a major city between roughly the 7th and 10th centuries.

Its buildings are known for clean geometric facades, long friezes and repeating masks of the rain god Chac. Getting here from Merida is simple by car, tour or hired driver, and like most Yucatan archaeological sites it charges two separate entrance fees at the gate. Plan two to three hours on site, then use the rest of the day for the smaller Puuc Route sites down the road.

What to see at Uxmal

Uxmal is compact enough to see the highlights on foot in a couple of hours, but detailed enough to reward a slower visit. These are the buildings most people come for.

The Pyramid of the Magician

The first structure you meet is the Pyramid of the Magician (Piramide del Adivino), and it is unlike almost any other Mayan pyramid. Instead of straight edges, it has rounded, oval sides that give it a soft, distinctive silhouette. Local legend ties it to a dwarf magician said to have built it overnight, which is where the name comes from. You can no longer climb it, but its scale and shape make it the signature image of the whole site.

The Nunnery Quadrangle

Just behind the pyramid sits the Nunnery Quadrangle (Cuadrangulo de las Monjas), four long buildings arranged around a wide courtyard. The name was given by the Spanish, who thought the many small rooms looked like a convent; it had nothing to do with nuns. The facades here are the reason many people rank Uxmal above other Yucatan sites for architecture, with intricate latticework, serpents, and Chac masks running the full length of each wall.

The Governor's Palace

Raised on a broad artificial platform, the Governor's Palace (Palacio del Gobernador) is often called one of the masterpieces of Maya architecture. Its long facade carries a mosaic frieze made of thousands of cut stones, and the building is aligned to mark the movements of Venus. Nearby you will also find the House of the Turtles, named for the carved turtles along its cornice, and the Great Pyramid, which you can climb for a wide view over the site and the surrounding jungle.

How to get to Uxmal from Merida

Merida is the natural base for Uxmal, and the two make an easy pairing. The drive south is about 80 km and takes roughly 1 to 1.5 hours depending on traffic and stops. You have a few options for Uxmal from Merida:

  • Rent a car: the most flexible choice, and it lets you continue onto the Puuc Route in the same day. The roads are paved and well signed.
  • Guided day tour: plenty of operators run Uxmal tours from Merida, often bundling the evening light show or a couple of Puuc sites. Good if you would rather not drive.
  • Colectivo or bus: budget-friendly public transport runs from Merida toward Uxmal, though schedules are limited and it takes longer, so confirm return times before you set out.

Uxmal tickets and fees

As at Chichen Itza and other Yucatan sites, Uxmal charges two separate entrance fees: a federal fee collected by INAH (the national institute that manages archaeological sites) and a Yucatan state fee. You pay both at the entrance, and together they make up the full cost of admission. Because these amounts are adjusted periodically, we do not print a figure here; check the current rates when you buy your ticket, and bring some cash in pesos in case card payment is unavailable. There is usually a separate charge for a professional video camera and for the evening show if it is running.

Best time to visit Uxmal

The best time to visit Uxmal is the dry season, roughly November to March, when temperatures are more comfortable and rain is less likely. Whatever the month, come as close to opening as you can. Early morning means softer light for photos, cooler air for walking the open platforms, and far fewer people in your frames. The Yucatan sun is strong by late morning, so bring water, a hat, sunscreen, and sturdy shoes for uneven stone. If you plan to add the Puuc Route, an early start is what makes a full day realistic.

The evening light show

Uxmal has long been known for an evening sound and light show that lights up the main buildings after dark. It is a memorable way to see the site, but the show has been paused at various times, so confirm it before you plan around it. Before planning your day around it, check that it is currently running and what time it starts, either through your tour operator or when you buy your daytime ticket. If it is on, note that the on-site amounts can differ from the daytime fee.

Uxmal vs Chichen Itza: which should you visit?

Most first-time visitors ask this. The two sites offer different experiences. Chichen Itza is larger, more famous, and home to the iconic El Castillo pyramid; it is also far busier, with crowds and vendors filling the main plazas by mid-morning. Uxmal is smaller and quieter, with architecture that many travelers find more beautiful up close, and you can often wander long stretches nearly alone.

If you only have time for one and want the marquee name, choose Chichen Itza. If you care about Puuc craftsmanship, calmer visits, and closer access to the buildings, choose Uxmal. If you can, do both on separate days from different bases; Chichen Itza pairs well with Valladolid, while Uxmal pairs best with Merida and the Puuc Route.

The Puuc Route near Uxmal

Uxmal is the gateway to the Puuc Route (Ruta Puuc), a short chain of smaller Mayan sites that share the same refined architectural style and see even fewer visitors. Within a short drive south you will find Kabah, known for its wall of hundreds of Chac masks; Sayil, with its long multi-level palace; the small site of Xlapak; and Labna, famous for its ornate stone arch. Many travelers also add the Loltun caves nearby. None take long on their own, so with an early start you can see several in a day. It is one of the best ways to escape the crowds entirely and understand why Puuc architecture is so admired.

Common questions

Can you climb the pyramids at Uxmal?

You cannot climb the Pyramid of the Magician. However, the Great Pyramid is currently open to climb and rewards you with a wide view over the site, and other platforms are accessible. Rules can change, so follow the signs and staff guidance on the day.

How long do you need at Uxmal?

Two to three hours covers the main buildings comfortably. If you add the Puuc Route sites of Kabah, Sayil, Xlapak, and Labna, plan a full day and start early.

Is Uxmal worth it compared to Chichen Itza?

Yes, especially if you value quieter visits and detailed architecture. Chichen Itza is bigger and more famous; Uxmal is calmer and, for many, more beautiful up close. Doing both on separate days is ideal.

Do I need a guide?

No, the site is walkable on your own with signage. A licensed guide at the entrance or a guided tour from Merida adds context on the Puuc style, the Chac masks, and the astronomy of the Governor's Palace.
