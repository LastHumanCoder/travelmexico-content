The Best Day Trips from Mexico City

Pyramids, canals, colonial towns and market towns, all within a couple of hours of the capital.

The short answer

The best Mexico City day trips are Teotihuacan for the pyramids, Xochimilco for the canals, Puebla and Cholula for colonial streets and food, and Tepoztlan for a magic town with a clifftop pyramid hike. Most sit within about two hours of the city and need only a single full day.

You could spend a week in the capital without running out of things to do, yet some of the best experiences in central Mexico are a short ride outside it: pyramids, canals, a colonial city with the country's most famous kitchen, and a mountain town with a clifftop pyramid hike. All of them fit inside a two-hour radius.

These are the day trips we send travelers on, with what each one is, how to get there and how long to allow. Teotihuacan and Xochimilco come first because everyone asks about them. Puebla, Cholula and Tepoztlan follow, then a note on Taxco and Valle de Bravo for people with more days. Every one of them rewards an early alarm. For the capital itself, see our Mexico City guide.

Teotihuacan: the pyramids

Teotihuacan is the one most people picture when they imagine a day trip from the capital. It is a vast pre Aztec city, laid out along a wide avenue with the Pyramid of the Sun and the Pyramid of the Moon anchoring each end. Walking the site gives you a sense of scale that photos never quite capture. It is one of the most impressive archaeological sites in the country.

Why go: history on a monumental scale, and it is close enough to fold into almost any itinerary. Whether you can currently climb the pyramids changes from time to time, so check the current rules on arrival rather than counting on it.

How to get there: the site sits roughly 30 miles northeast of the city. Buses run from the Terminal Central del Norte (the North bus terminal) directly to the gates, or you can take an organized tour or private driver, which many travelers prefer for the early start. How long: allow a half day minimum, a full day if you want to explore slowly and beat the midday sun. Go early, bring water, a hat and sunscreen, because there is little shade. Xochimilco: the canals

At the southern edge of the city, Xochimilco is a network of canals left from the lake system the Aztecs built their capital on. You ride a trajinera, a flat, brightly painted boat, poled slowly along the water while boats selling food, drinks and live music drift past. It is festive, social and completely different from anything else on this list.

Why go: it is fun, relaxed and works brilliantly for groups, families or anyone wanting a slower afternoon. Boats are hired by the hour and the rate is set per boat rather than per person, so it is best value with a group; agree the price and the length of the ride before you set off. How to get there: it is within the city, reachable by Metro and light rail or by taxi and rideshare, which is simpler if you are short on time. How long: a half day is plenty, and it pairs well with a morning elsewhere in the south. Full details are in our Xochimilco guide.

Puebla and Cholula: colonial city, food and the Great Pyramid

A puebla day trip from mexico city gives you two places in one. Puebla is a handsome colonial city famous for its tiled facades, its cathedral and a food scene that many Mexicans consider among the best in the country. This is the home of mole poblano and chiles en nogada, so come hungry.

Just next door, Cholula is home to the Great Pyramid of Cholula, which by base volume is one of the largest pyramids in the world. Much of it is a grass covered hill today, with a church built on top and tunnels running through the base. The combination of a Spanish church crowning an ancient pyramid sums up central Mexico neatly.

Why go: colonial architecture, standout food and a pyramid unlike any other in Mexico, all in a walkable day. How to get there: comfortable buses run frequently from the capital, and the ride takes roughly two hours each way, so it is doable in a day but works even better as an overnight if you can spare it. How long: a full day, and an early bus makes all the difference. Because you are covering two towns, decide in advance whether food or ruins is the priority so you are not rushing both.

Tepoztlan: magic town and pyramid hike

Tepoztlan is one of Mexico's designated Pueblos Magicos, or magic towns, and it earns the label. Set beneath dramatic cliffs south of the city, it has a lively weekend market, artisan shops and a laid back mountain feel. The signature experience is the hike up to the Tepozteco pyramid, a small temple perched high on the ridge above town.

Why go: a beautiful setting, a rewarding hike and a great market for crafts and street food. The trail up to the pyramid is steep and rocky and takes real effort, so wear proper shoes, carry water and give yourself time; it is worth it for the views over the valley. How to get there: buses head south from the capital and the trip takes roughly one and a half to two hours. How long: a full day lets you hike in the cooler morning and browse the market and eat in the afternoon. Tepoztlan is one of the best Mexico City day trips if you want a mix of nature and town in a single outing.

Taxco and Valle de Bravo: two more to consider

If you have extra days, two more spots are worth a mention. Taxco is a silver mining town that spills down a steep hillside southwest of the capital, all white buildings, red roofs and winding lanes, with a famous baroque church at its heart. It is a longer drive, so it suits travelers who do not mind more time on the road for a striking, one of a kind town.

Valle de Bravo sits to the west around a lake and is the classic weekend escape from the city, popular for sailing, paragliding and pine forest walks. It is far enough that many people make it an overnight rather than a rushed single day. Both are better as relaxed additions than as your first day trip, but they show how much range central Mexico has once you look past the obvious.

A quick word on markets

You do not have to leave the city to feel like you have traveled. Some of the best markets in mexico city are day trips in miniature, easy to reach and full of food, crafts and daily life. La Ciudadela is the go to for handicrafts from around the country, Mercado de Coyoacan is great for a relaxed lunch of tostadas in a leafy neighborhood, and the sprawling Mercado de la Merced is an experience in itself. On the day trips above, the markets in Tepoztlan and Puebla are highlights in their own right, so build in time to wander and eat.

How to fit day trips into a longer stay

If you are here for the better part of a week, a simple rhythm works well: spend your first couple of days in the capital itself, then alternate day trips with lighter city days so you are not on a bus every morning. A balanced mexico city itinerary 5 days might pair Teotihuacan early in the trip, Xochimilco on a slower afternoon, and one bigger outing to Puebla or Tepoztlan, leaving room for neighborhoods, museums and food back in town. Always factor in traffic; leaving the city early and returning before evening rush saves real time. Common questions

What is the best day trip from Mexico City?

Teotihuacan is the most popular for its pyramids, but Xochimilco, Puebla and Tepoztlan are all excellent depending on whether you want history, a festive boat ride, a colonial city or a mountain town. If you only have time for one, choose by the experience you most want.

Can I do Teotihuacan and Xochimilco in one day?

It is not ideal because they sit on opposite sides of the city and each deserves its own time. If you must combine two things, pair a nearby market or neighborhood with one main destination rather than rushing two big sites in a single day.

Do I need a tour or can I go on my own?

Most of these are easy to reach independently by bus or, for Xochimilco, by Metro and rideshare. Tours and private drivers mainly buy you an earlier start and less planning, which some travelers value for Teotihuacan and Puebla.

How much do day trips cost?

Costs vary by transport, whether you take a tour, and current entry rates, which change over time. Buy tickets on arrival or online and check current prices close to your trip rather than relying on old figures.
