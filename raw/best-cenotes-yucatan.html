The Best Cenotes in the Yucatan Peninsula

A specialist's roundup of the Yucatan's finest natural swimming holes, grouped by where you are based.

The short answer

The best cenotes in Yucatan cluster around three bases. From Valladolid, visit Suytun, Oxman, Zaci and Ik Kil. From Merida, tour the Cuzama and Homun cenotes. From Tulum, swim Gran Cenote, Dos Ojos and Cenote Calavera. Go early, bring biodegradable sunscreen and water shoes.

Cenotes are natural sinkholes where the peninsula's limestone crust has collapsed to reveal the freshwater river system underneath. There are thousands of them, so picking the best cenotes in Yucatan means picking the ones that are accessible, well cared for and worth your morning.

The useful way to choose is by where you are sleeping. A cenote near Valladolid feels nothing like one near Merida, and the cave systems near Tulum are their own world, so this guide sorts the standouts into three day-trip clusters. Wherever you are based, there is a swim within about an hour.

What a cenote is

Cenotes come in three broad types, and knowing them helps you pick the right one for how you want to spend the day. Open cenotes are exposed to the sky like a large pond or a sunken pool, often ringed by hanging tree roots. Semi-open cenotes sit partly under a rock overhang, so half the pool is in shade and half catches the light. Cave cenotes are fully enclosed, entered by a staircase into a chamber lit by shafts of daylight and, sometimes, artificial lighting. Open cenotes tend to be warmer and easier for families; cave cenotes are cooler, more dramatic and where most snorkeling and diving happens.

All of them share the same water: cool, extraordinarily clear freshwater filtered through limestone. Visibility often runs many meters, which is exactly why the peninsula is a global destination for cavern and cave diving. The water can feel cold when you first get in, so give yourself a minute before you judge it.

Best cenotes near Valladolid

The colonial town of Valladolid is the single best base for cenote hopping, and it makes an easy stop on any Yucatan road trip itinerary. Several of the most photographed cenotes near Valladolid are within a short drive or even walking distance of the main square. See our full Valladolid guide for where to stay and eat between swims.

Suytun

Suytun is the cave cenote behind the famous image of a stone platform reaching into the water while a single beam of light drops through the roof. It is a semi-enclosed cavern a short drive east of town. The light beam is strongest around midday and only on sunny days, so arrive early to beat both the crowds and the tour buses that line up for that one shot.

Oxman (Hacienda San Lorenzo)

Oxman is a deep, round open cenote draped in curtains of tree roots, with a rope swing and a hacienda setting. It is a favorite for an unhurried swim and a lounge in the sun, and it feels less rushed than the cave cenotes.

Zaci

Zaci sits right in the center of Valladolid, so you can walk to it. It is a large semi-open cenote with a partial cave roof and a walkway around the rim. If you only have a couple of hours in town, this is the convenient one.

Ik Kil (near Chichen Itza)

Ik Kil is the big circular open cenote most people pair with a morning at the ruins, since it sits just minutes from Chichen Itza. Vines hang the full depth of the wall down to the water. It gets very busy midday because of its position on the Chichen Itza tour circuit, so swim it early or late.

Best cenotes near Tulum

The Riviera Maya side of the peninsula is where the flooded cave systems are, and it is the heartland of cenote diving Yucatan. The cenotes near Tulum are generally cave and cavern sites with world-class visibility, which is why divers travel here from all over the world. You do not have to dive to enjoy them: snorkeling over these systems is spectacular in its own right.

Gran Cenote

Gran Cenote Tulum is the classic introduction, a bright, shallow cavern-and-open system a short drive from town where snorkelers share the water with small turtles and where cavern divers begin their cave training. The clarity is remarkable and the shallow areas make it approachable for less confident swimmers.

Dos Ojos

Dos Ojos, meaning "two eyes," is one of the most celebrated cavern-diving and snorkeling systems in the world, with two connected pools and long lines of stalactites underwater. It is a headline stop for anyone serious about cenote diving Yucatan, and the snorkeling over the entrances is excellent.

Cenote Calavera

Cenote Calavera, the "skull" cenote, is a smaller open pit with three holes in the roof, one large and two small, and a jump-in entry that regulars love. It is close to Tulum town and a good pick when you want a quick, fun swim rather than a full excursion.

Best cenotes near Merida

Travelers based in the Yucatan capital often assume the cenotes are all far away, but some of the most memorable are an easy drive south. The cenotes near Merida around the villages of Cuzama and Homun are a cluster of gorgeous cave and semi-open cenotes, several of them reached the traditional way by a horse-drawn rail cart, or truck, along old henequen plantation tracks. Because they take a little effort to reach, they stay quieter than the headline sites. Use our Merida guide to plan the town side of the trip.

Homun in particular has become a favorite for a slow day of hopping between several small enclosed cenotes, climbing down ladders into cool blue chambers one after another. It is a lovely contrast to the busier cave cenotes on the tourist circuit and a reminder that the best cenotes in Yucatan are not always the famous ones.

Swimming, snorkeling or diving

Most visitors come to swim, and nearly all the cenotes above are set up for it, with staircases, platforms and often life vests available. Snorkeling adds a lot in the clear cave systems near Tulum, where you can float over rock formations and watch the light play through the water. Diving is a separate commitment: cavern diving stays within sight of daylight and is open to certified open-water divers, while full cave diving is a specialist qualification and should only ever be done with a certified cave guide. If cenote diving Yucatan is your reason for the trip, base yourself near Tulum where the operators and training are concentrated.

What to bring and how to behave

The single most important item is biodegradable, reef-safe sunscreen, and many cenotes will ask you to rinse off regular sunscreen and bug spray before entering because the chemicals harm this fragile freshwater ecosystem. Bring water shoes for slippery limestone steps, a quick-dry towel, and cash, since smaller sites may not take cards. A waterproof phone pouch or action camera helps in the low light of the caves.

Etiquette matters here. These are living ecosystems and, for the Maya, sacred places. Do not touch stalactites or cave walls, do not stand on or break formations, take all trash out with you, and keep noise down inside the caves. Arriving early does more than beat the crowds: it protects the calm that makes these places special. Treat every cenote as somewhere you are a guest.

Practical notes

Which cenote has the famous light-beam photo?

That is Suytun, near Valladolid. The single shaft of light hitting the stone platform is strongest around midday on sunny days, so arrive early to line up before the tour buses.

Do I need to be able to dive to enjoy the cenotes?

No. Almost all of these cenotes are set up for swimming, and life vests are widely available. Snorkeling is a nice extra in the clear cave systems near Tulum, and diving is optional for certified divers.

How much do cenotes cost to enter?

Each cenote sets its own entrance fee and hours, and they change, so check the current rate and pay on arrival. Bring cash, as smaller village cenotes near Merida may not accept cards.

What should I never do inside a cenote?

Do not wear regular sunscreen or bug spray in the water, do not touch or break the cave formations, and take all your trash with you. Reef-safe products and quiet, careful behavior keep these ecosystems healthy.
