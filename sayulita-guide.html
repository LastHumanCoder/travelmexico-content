Sayulita: The Complete Destination Guide

A bohemian surf town on the Riviera Nayarit, about 45 minutes north of Puerto Vallarta.

The short answer

The best things to do in Sayulita are learning to surf on its gentle beach break, wandering the colorful town and its shops, relaxing on the main beach and quieter Playa de los Muertos, and taking a boat day trip to the Marietas Islands. Plan on two to four days.

Sayulita is a small surf town on the Riviera Nayarit, about 45 minutes north of Puerto Vallarta. Papel picado banners crisscross the cobbled streets, surfboards lean outside every other doorway and the whole town sits within a short walk of the sand.

The things to do in Sayulita cluster around the main beach, the town center and the water just offshore, which is why two to four days is plenty. Beginner surf lessons, slow beach afternoons, artisan shops and a boat day to the Marietas Islands fill the time without a car. It pairs well with inland Jalisco, Guadalajara and Tequila, or works as a base on its own.

Things to do in Sayulita

The town rewards slow mornings and unstructured afternoons, but a few experiences stand out. Nearly everything is within walking distance, so you can mix and match without renting a car.

Surf the beach break

Surfing is the heartbeat of Sayulita. The main beach has a forgiving, sandy-bottom break that is famous as one of the friendliest places on the Pacific coast to catch a first wave. Surf schools and board rentals line the beach, and instructors run group and private lessons throughout the day. More experienced surfers head to the north side or nearby breaks for cleaner, longer rides.

Relax on the beaches

Sayulita's main beach is the social center of town, with palapa restaurants, surfers, and vendors all sharing the same stretch of sand. For something quieter, walk about 15 to 20 minutes south through the local cemetery to Playa de los Muertos, a small, sheltered cove with calmer water that is better for swimming and snorkeling than the surf beach.

Wander the town and its shops

The town center is made for aimless wandering. Independent boutiques, Huichol beadwork, hammocks, and handmade goods fill the streets around the main plaza, and the food scene runs from taco stands and fish tacos to coffee roasters and sit-down restaurants. Evenings bring live music and a relaxed nightlife that stays true to the town's small-town scale.

Take a day trip to the Marietas Islands

The Marietas Islands, a protected national park in Banderas Bay, are the most popular boat day trip from Sayulita. Tours combine snorkeling, wildlife watching, and, when access is granted, a look at the famous hidden beach inside a collapsed crater. Access to the hidden beach is limited to protect the reef, so book through an authorized operator and check current rules before you go.

The surf scene, for beginners and beyond

Sayulita's reputation as a surf town is well earned. The main beach break is gentle and sandy, which makes it ideal for first-timers and kids, and the density of surf schools means you can usually book a lesson the same day. Boards are easy to rent by the hour or day. As you improve, the town works as a base for exploring nearby Riviera Nayarit breaks, some of which offer longer or more powerful waves. Conditions shift with the swell and season, so ask a local school what is working on the day you arrive.

Where to stay in Sayulita

Deciding where to stay in Sayulita comes down to how close you want to be to the action. Staying in or near the town center puts you steps from the beach, restaurants, and nightlife, which is lively but can be noisy at night. The hillsides above town and the north side offer quieter boutique hotels and rental homes with views, a short walk or drive from the center. Sayulita ranges from hostels and budget guesthouses to design-forward boutique stays, so there is something for most travelers. Book well ahead for the dry-season high season and holidays.

Sayulita vs Puerto Vallarta

Sayulita and Puerto Vallarta suit different travelers. Puerto Vallarta is a full-size resort city with an international airport, a long malecon boardwalk, big hotels, a wide range of restaurants and nightlife, and plenty for families and larger groups. Sayulita is a small boho beach town: walkable, artsy, surf-focused, and low-rise, with a fraction of the crowds and none of the resort scale. Many travelers do both, flying into Puerto Vallarta and using it as a hub, then spending a few slower days up the coast in Sayulita, and some continue inland to Guadalajara and Tequila to round out a Jalisco and Nayarit loop. If you want variety, nightlife, and convenience, lean toward Puerto Vallarta; if you want a laid-back surf town with character, choose Sayulita.

How to get to Sayulita

Most visitors arrive through Puerto Vallarta International Airport (PVR), which sits about 45 minutes south of Sayulita. From the airport you can take a private transfer or taxi, rent a car, or catch one of the frequent local buses that run up the coast toward Sayulita. The drive is straightforward on Highway 200. A Sayulita day trip from Puerto Vallarta is very doable if you are short on time, but the town rewards at least an overnight stay so you can enjoy a slow morning surf and a relaxed evening in town.

Best time to visit Sayulita

The dry season, roughly November through April, brings sunny days, warm temperatures, and generally calmer seas, and it is the most popular time to visit. Expect the biggest crowds and highest demand around the winter holidays and spring break, so reserve lodging early. The summer and early fall months are hotter and more humid, with a rainy season that usually brings short afternoon showers and greener surroundings, plus fewer visitors. Surf conditions vary year-round, so check the current swell for the style of waves you want.

Common questions

Is Sayulita good for beginner surfers?

Yes. The main beach has a gentle, sandy-bottom break that is one of the most beginner-friendly on the Pacific coast, with plenty of surf schools and board rentals right on the sand.

How far is Sayulita from Puerto Vallarta?

Sayulita is about 45 minutes north of Puerto Vallarta by car. You can reach it by private transfer, taxi, rental car, or local bus from the airport or the city.

How many days do you need in Sayulita?

Two to four days is enough to surf, enjoy the beaches, explore the town, and fit in a day trip such as the Marietas Islands. It also works as a longer, slow base on the Riviera Nayarit.

Do you need a car in Sayulita?

No. The town, beaches, and most restaurants are within walking distance. A car or tour is only useful for nearby breaks, day trips, or exploring more of the Riviera Nayarit.
