Tequila: The Town, the Distilleries and the Day Trip

Tequila is a small Jalisco town about an hour northwest of Guadalajara, wrapped in the blue agave fields that give the spirit its flavor. The fields and the old distilleries together form a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape, so the day is as much about scenery and history as the tasting at the end.

A Guadalajara to Tequila tour is the classic way to do it, with the tourist train and self-drive as the alternatives. Whichever you pick, the day has the same shape: out through the agave country, a distillery tour and tasting, then free time in tequila town Mexico itself, with its shaded plaza and tasting rooms. Leave the driving to someone else.

Why Tequila is worth the trip

Tequila is one of Mexico's Pueblos Magicos, and it earns the label. The setting gets you first: mile after mile of spiky blue agave in neat rows across red volcanic soil, framed by the dormant Tequila Volcano. This is the agave landscape UNESCO recognized as World Heritage, and it looks like nowhere else in Mexico. The drink can only legally be called tequila when it is made here in Jalisco, and a few neighboring areas, from the blue agave grown in exactly this ground.

The town itself is compact and easy to walk, built around a main plaza with a church, shaded benches, street snacks, and tasting rooms lining the streets. You will see the barrel-shaped cantarito cups, hear mariachi, and find everything from tiny family brands to the big global names. It is the kind of place where a tequila Mexico day trip turns into a slow afternoon without you quite deciding to let it.

What to do in Tequila

Take a distillery tour

The distillery tour is the main event, and there is a version for every taste. The big producers run polished visits with grounds you can explore, and a Jose Cuervo distillery tour is the best known, based at the historic La Rojena distillery in town. Family-run distilleries offer something smaller and more personal, often with the maestro walking you through it. For the best distillery tours in Tequila, the big houses give you scale and history and the small ones give you intimacy and detail. One of each makes a good day.

Walk the agave fields

Some tours include time in the fields, where a jimador demonstrates how the heavy agave heart, the pina, is harvested with a sharp round blade called a coa. Standing among the plants with the volcano behind them is the photo you came for.

Enjoy the town square

Leave time to simply be in the town. Wander the plaza, try a tasting flight at your own pace, pick up a bottle you cannot find at home, and eat well. If craft spirits are your thing, Tequila pairs naturally with the mezcal country around Oaxaca City for a wider tasting trip.

How to get to Tequila from Guadalajara

Tequila sits roughly 40 miles northwest of Guadalajara, and the drive takes about an hour to an hour and a half depending on traffic and your exact starting point. There are three practical ways to make the trip.

By organized tour

A guided Guadalajara to Tequila tour is the most popular choice. Transport, a guide, and the distillery stops are arranged for you, so nobody has to worry about driving after a tasting. Tours typically bundle one or two distillery visits, time in the town, and often a stop in the agave fields, and a good guide adds the history that turns a tasting into an education. If you want the day handled end to end, our partner can arrange a tour along with your Guadalajara hotel and transfers.

By the tequila train

The tequila express train is a Jalisco institution and a memorable way to travel. Several rail experiences depart from Guadalajara and roll through the agave country to distilleries near or in Tequila, with tastings, food, and music on board and a distillery visit built into the day. Names, routes, and schedules vary between operators and change over time, so book ahead and confirm the current departure point, timetable, and inclusions before you go.

By self-drive

If you have a car, the drive is straightforward on the highway northwest out of Guadalajara, with parking in and around town. The one firm rule is to sort out a designated driver, because the whole point of the day involves tasting alcohol. Self-driving gives you full control of your timing and choice of distilleries. What a distillery tour includes

Most tours follow the same journey from plant to glass, which is what makes them satisfying. You start with the blue agave and learn how the pina is harvested and cooked, traditionally in ovens or autoclaves, to turn its starches into sugar. From there you see the crushing and fermentation, then distillation in copper or stainless stills, and finally aging in oak barrels that separates a crisp blanco from a golden reposado or anejo. It ends with a guided tasting where you compare those styles side by side.

Tour length, group size, and inclusions all vary by distillery and by whether you booked a big-house visit or a boutique one, so reserve ahead for the popular names, especially on weekends. Prices differ between operators, so confirm current tour and tasting costs directly with the distillery or your operator rather than relying on an old figure.

Tastings and responsible visiting

Tequila is best sipped and appreciated, not rushed. Take your time with the flights, drink water alongside, and eat during the day, and you will enjoy the flavors far more. Because every version of this day involves alcohol, transport is the thing to get right: let a tour or the train do the driving, or commit to a designated driver if you self-drive. Buying a bottle or two to take home is part of the fun, so check your airline's rules for liquids in checked baggage before you load up.

Best time to visit

The best time to visit Tequila is during the dry season, roughly November to May, when clear skies show off the agave fields and the volcano. The summer rainy season turns the landscape lush green but brings afternoon showers, so time outdoor stops for the morning. Weekdays are noticeably calmer than weekends and Mexican holidays, when the town and popular distilleries fill up. Starting reasonably early gives you an unhurried day and the best light for the fields.

Where to base yourself

Most travelers visit Tequila as a day trip and base themselves in Guadalajara, which has the widest choice of hotels, the best food and nightlife, and the easiest connections to the tours and the train. If you would rather slow down, there are boutique hotels in and around Tequila itself, including properties on distillery estates, that let you enjoy the town after the day trippers leave. For a coastal add-on, the surf town of Sayulita pairs well with time in Guadalajara and Tequila.

Before you go

How far is Tequila from Guadalajara?

Tequila is about 40 miles northwest of Guadalajara, and the road trip takes roughly an hour to an hour and a half depending on traffic and where in the city you start.

Do you need a tour, or can you go on your own?

You can do either. A guided tour or the tequila train handles transport and distillery stops for you, while self-driving with a designated driver lets you choose your own pace and distilleries.

Can you visit the big-name distilleries?

Yes. Major producers, including a Jose Cuervo distillery tour at the historic La Rojena distillery in town, run organized visits, and smaller family distilleries offer more personal tours. Reserve ahead for the popular names.

How much time do you need in Tequila?

Plan a full day. Allow travel each way plus a few hours for one or two distillery tours and time to wander the town square at your own pace.
