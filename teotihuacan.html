Teotihuacan: Visiting the Pyramids from Mexico City

Everything you need for an easy, early-start day among the pyramids.

The short answer

A Teotihuacan day trip is one of the best half-day escapes from Mexico City. The ancient city sits about an hour northeast and is home to the Pyramid of the Sun, the Pyramid of the Moon, and the Avenue of the Dead. Go by bus, car, or organized tour, and arrive early.

Roughly 30 miles northeast of Mexico City, Teotihuacan was one of the largest cities in the ancient world, and the Avenue of the Dead still runs between two pyramids big enough to make photographs feel dishonest. Most visitors do it as a morning and are back in the city for a late lunch.

Three ways get you there: the public bus from Terminal Central del Norte, a car or private driver, or a guided tour that bundles transport and a guide. Every specialist gives the same advice regardless of which you pick, and it is worth repeating once. Start early. The gate opens in the morning, the light is soft, the groups are thin, and the high-altitude sun has not yet turned the open plaza into a furnace.

What Teotihuacan is

Teotihuacan is an ancient city, not a single monument. At its height it may have housed more than 100,000 people, centuries before the Aztecs arrived and gave it the name we still use, meaning roughly "the place where the gods were created." The layout is built around one grand central axis, and understanding three landmarks is enough to make the whole site click into place.

The Avenue of the Dead is the wide ceremonial road that runs through the heart of the city and connects the major structures. Walking its length gives you the best sense of the scale. The Pyramid of the Sun is the giant at the center, one of the largest pyramids in the Americas and the structure most people picture when they imagine Teotihuacan. The Pyramid of the Moon anchors the northern end of the avenue and frames a beautiful view back down the road. Near the southern entrance, the Ciudadela plaza holds the Temple of the Feathered Serpent, decorated with carved stone heads that reward a close look. If you are also planning to see the Maya ruins of the Yucatan, Teotihuacan makes a fascinating contrast with a site like Chichen Itza, built by a different civilization far to the southeast.

How to get to Teotihuacan

There are three practical ways to reach the site, and the right one depends on your budget, your comfort with public transport, and whether you want a guide.

By bus from Terminal Central del Norte

The cheapest and most independent option is the bus. Head to the Terminal Central del Norte (the North Bus Terminal), reachable on Mexico City's Metro. Buses to the pyramids run frequently throughout the morning; look for the service marked for "Los Piramides" or Teotihuacan rather than the town of San Juan Teotihuacan, and tell the counter you want the archaeological zone so you are dropped at one of the site gates. The ride takes about an hour each way. Keep your return ticket or note the schedule, and hold onto your belongings as you would on any city transport.

By car or private driver

Driving gives you the most control over your timing, which matters when the goal is to arrive at opening. From Mexico City the route runs northeast on the highway toward the site, and there is paid parking at the gates. If you would rather not drive in Mexico City traffic, a private driver for the morning is a comfortable middle ground and lets you leave the moment the gates open.

By organized tour

A guided teotihuacan tour from Mexico City is the easiest option and the most popular. Transport, a knowledgeable guide, and a set schedule are handled for you, and many tours pair the pyramids with a nearby stop such as the Basilica of Guadalupe or a tasting of pulque or obsidian crafts. A guide adds real context to what you are seeing, which is easy to miss on your own. Tickets and entry logistics

Teotihuacan tickets are sold at the entrance gates, so there is usually no need to book admission in advance. Buy your ticket on arrival at the gate you reach. Admission is set by Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH), and rates and conditions can change, so check the current admission and any advance-reservation requirements with INAH before your visit rather than relying on a figure you read somewhere online. Bring some cash, as small vendors and parking are easiest to pay in pesos.

The site has several gates spaced along its perimeter, numbered around the archaeological zone. Whichever gate you enter, the Avenue of the Dead links everything, so you can simply start walking and work your way from one pyramid to the other. There are restrooms and small shops near the main gates.

A note on climbing the pyramids

Rules about climbing the pyramids have changed over the years, and access can be restricted to protect the structures or for maintenance. Do not assume you will be able to climb to the top on any given day. Confirm current access when you buy your ticket or with INAH beforehand, and always follow the posted signs and staff instructions on site. Even when climbing is limited, walking the Avenue of the Dead and standing at the base of the pyramids is a remarkable experience on its own.

Best time to visit Teotihuacan

The best time to visit Teotihuacan is early in the morning, as close to opening as you can manage. There is very little shade across the open plaza, and Teotihuacan sits at high altitude, so the midday sun is strong and tiring. An early start means cooler air, gentler light for photos, and far fewer tour groups on the avenue. Weekdays are generally quieter than weekends, when Mexico City families visit.

Across the year, the dry season from roughly November to April brings the most reliable clear mornings. The summer rainy season can deliver dramatic skies but also afternoon downpours, another reason to be on site early and done by lunch. Whatever the season, an early Teotihuacan day trip beats the heat and the crowds.

How long you need

Half a day is enough for most visitors. Plan on about two to three hours walking the site itself, plus roughly an hour of travel each way. That makes Teotihuacan a comfortable morning outing you can pair with a relaxed afternoon back in the capital. If you love ancient history and want to take your time at the on-site museum and the smaller residential compounds, you can happily stretch it to most of a day.

What to bring

Sun protection

Hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen are essential. Shade is scarce and the high-altitude sun is intense.

Water

Carry more than you think you need. Walking the avenue and the plazas is thirsty work.

Good shoes

Comfortable, closed walking shoes for uneven stone, gravel, and long distances between structures.

Cash in pesos

Useful for your ticket, parking, and vendors near the gates, where cards are not always taken.

Where to base yourself

Almost everyone visits Teotihuacan as a day trip from Mexico City, which has the widest choice of hotels and the easiest transport links to the site. Staying in a central neighborhood puts you close to the bus terminal and to countless other things to do before and after your pyramid morning. Practical notes

Can you visit Teotihuacan on your own without a tour?

Yes. Many travelers take the bus from the Terminal Central del Norte or drive themselves, buy a ticket at the gate, and explore independently. A guide adds context, but it is not required to enjoy the site.

Can you still climb the pyramids?

Access has changed over time and can be restricted on any given day to protect the structures. Do not assume you can climb to the top. Confirm current access with INAH or at the ticket gate, and follow posted signs and staff on site.

How much time should you spend at Teotihuacan?

Around two to three hours on site is enough to walk the Avenue of the Dead and see both pyramids, plus travel time each way. It works well as a half-day trip from Mexico City.

What is the best time of day to go?

Early morning, right at opening. It is cooler, the light is better for photos, and the tour groups arrive later, so the site is far quieter.
